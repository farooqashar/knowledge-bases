# Campus and Buildings

MIT's campus runs along the Cambridge side of the Charles River, across from Boston's Back Bay. Like its departments, its buildings are known by **numbers**, and much of the main campus is connected indoors, so you can cross it in winter without going outside.

## How building numbers work

- A room number reads **building-room**: **10-250** is room 250 in Building 10. The first digit of the room usually gives the floor.
- Buildings are numbered roughly by area: the original main group is 1 through 10; **E** buildings are east of campus, **W** buildings are west, **N** are north and **NE** northeast.
- Many buildings also have names, but students mostly use the numbers.

## Landmarks

- **The Great Dome (Building 10):** the symbol of MIT, overlooking **Killian Court**, where Commencement is held. A favorite stage for hacks.
- **The Infinite Corridor:** the main hallway through Buildings 7, 3, 10, 4 and 8, about 251 meters (823 feet) long. It connects much of the main campus.
- **MITHenge:** a few days each year, around mid-November and late January, the setting sun lines up with the Infinite Corridor and shines down its whole length. Crowds gather to watch.
- **Lobby 7:** the domed entrance on Massachusetts Avenue, the main front door to campus.
- **The Stata Center (Building 32):** Frank Gehry's tilted, colorful building, opened in 2004, home to computer science and AI labs.
- **The Green Building (Building 54):** I. M. Pei's tall Earth sciences tower, famous for the Tetris hack played on its windows.
- **Building 20:** a "temporary" wooden building from World War II that housed the Radiation Laboratory's radar work and later many labs. It became a legend for flexible, inventive research before being demolished in 1998; the Stata Center stands on its site.
- **The Harvard Bridge (Mass Ave bridge):** connects MIT to Boston and is marked in **smoots** (see [Traditions](traditions.md)).
