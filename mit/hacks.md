# Hacks

At MIT, a **hack** is a clever, harmless, usually anonymous prank, typically installed overnight and discovered by the campus in the morning. The best hacks are funny, technically impressive and leave no damage. They're unsanctioned, and hackers traditionally follow an informal code: be safe, cause no damage, leave things as you found them.

The word is older than its computer meaning, and MIT's culture of "hacking" helped give it that later meaning.

## Famous hacks

| Year | Hack |
|---|---|
| 1958 | The Harvard Bridge is measured in **smoots** (see [Traditions](traditions.md)) |
| 1979 | A fiberglass steer from a local steakhouse appears on the Great Dome |
| 1982 | A weather balloon marked "MIT" inflates out of the turf mid-game at the Harvard–Yale football game |
| 1991 | A working **fire hydrant** turned into a drinking fountain outside the biggest lecture hall, a nod to the saying that an MIT education is "like drinking from a fire hose" |
| 1994 | A replica **MIT police car** on top of the Great Dome |
| 1999 | The Great Dome dressed as **R2-D2** for the release of *Star Wars: Episode I* |
| 2003 | A full-size **Wright Flyer** replica above campus for the centennial of flight |
| 2006 | A **fire truck** on the Great Dome, and the "liberation" of Caltech's Civil War-era cannon, which was shipped across the country to Cambridge |
| 2007 | Harvard's John Harvard statue dressed as **Master Chief** from *Halo* |
| 2009 | A replica **Red Line subway car** attached to the side of the Great Dome |
| 2012 | **Tetris** played on the windows of the Green Building, turning the tower into a giant game screen |
| 2019 | The Great Dome painted as **Captain America's shield** for *Avengers: Endgame* |

## Related terms

- **Roof and tunnel hacking:** exploring MIT's rooftops and steam tunnels. Also unsanctioned.
- **IHTFP:** see the [Glossary](glossary.md); it often shows up in hacks.
