# Course Numbers

At MIT, departments and majors go by numbers. A student is "Course 6," not "a computer science major," and a class like Physics I is "8.01" (said "eight oh one"). The number before the dot is the department; the number after it identifies the class.

Some programs use letters instead: **STS** (Science, Technology, and Society), **CMS** (Comparative Media Studies), **MAS** (Media Arts and Sciences, the Media Lab), **HST** (Health Sciences and Technology, with Harvard) and **IDSS** (Data, Systems, and Society).

**Missing numbers.** Gaps like 13, 19 and 23 are departments that were merged into others or renamed over the decades. Old course numbers live on in alumni conversations long after the department changed.

## Course 6 majors

Course 6 is MIT's largest department, so its majors have their own numbers:

| Major | Name |
|---|---|
| 6-3 | Computer Science and Engineering |
| 6-4 | Artificial Intelligence and Decision Making |
| 6-5 | Electrical Engineering with Computing |
| 6-7 | Computer Science and Molecular Biology (with Biology) |
| 6-9 | Computation and Cognition (with Brain and Cognitive Sciences) |
| 6-14 | Computer Science, Economics, and Data Science (with Economics) |

EECS restructured its majors in the early 2020s, adding 6-4 and replacing older numbers such as 6-1 (Electrical Engineering) and 6-2 (Electrical Engineering and Computer Science). Alumni from before then may still use the old numbers.

## Reading a class number

- **8.01**: Physics department (8), class 01. Many intro classes end in low numbers.
- **18.06**: Mathematics (18), Linear Algebra.
- **6.1010**: Course 6 moved to four-digit numbers in its restructuring, so older names like "6.009" now have new numbers.
- **Letters after the number** usually mark versions of a class, such as **8.01L** (a slower-paced version of 8.01).
- **J** marks a class offered jointly by two departments, listed under both numbers.
