# Academics

How undergraduate study at MIT is structured: the requirements every student shares, how classes are counted, and the programs that shape the experience.

## General Institute Requirements (GIRs)

Every undergraduate, in every major, completes the GIRs:

- **Science Core (6 subjects):** two calculus (18.01, 18.02), two physics (8.01, 8.02), one chemistry (such as 5.111 or 3.091) and one biology (such as 7.012), each with alternate versions.
- **REST (2 subjects):** "Restricted Electives in Science and Technology," which build on the Science Core. At most one can come from your own department.
- **Institute Lab (1 subject):** a hands-on laboratory class.
- **HASS (8 subjects):** humanities, arts and social sciences, split into a distribution across those areas, a concentration of 3 or 4 subjects in one field, and electives.
- **Communication (4 subjects):** two communication-intensive HASS classes (**CI-H**) and two in the major (**CI-M**), spread across the four years.
- **Physical education:** a swim test plus PE classes.

## Units and grades

- Classes are measured in **units**, roughly the hours of work per week. A typical class is **12 units** (for example, 3 hours of lecture, 1 of recitation and 8 of homework).
- GPA is on a **5.0 scale** (A = 5).
- The first semester is graded **pass/no record**: classes you don't pass simply don't appear on your transcript. This eases the transition into MIT's pace.

## Programs that define the experience

- **UROP** (Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program): undergraduates join real research labs, for pay or credit. Most students do at least one.
- **IAP** (Independent Activities Period): a four-week January term, first held in 1971, with hundreds of short, optional classes, from welding to Mystery Hunt.
- **OpenCourseWare (OCW):** MIT publishes materials from most of its classes free online at [ocw.mit.edu](https://ocw.mit.edu), a project started in the early 2000s.
- **Psets**: problem sets, the weekly homework that defines student life. Collaborating on psets, within each class's rules, is a core part of the culture.
